What began that day was meant to be the first of many. It wasn't. There has been no second attempt since, and the festival itself carries no name on any calendar the House keeps — only the shape of what's missing.

The date was Summer 28th. It is not blank on the calendar anymore — the House marks it now, as the Lantern Festival, though it does not call it what it is. A night of remembrance for worlds and loved ones lost, decorated lanterns sent floating into the sky and drifting from the Boardwalk on small boats, carrying prayers. Nobody who lights one has to know why the date was chosen. It's enough that it was.

The following is recorded here, as it is recorded elsewhere in the House, not to explain what happened, but so that it isn't forgotten who was here.
